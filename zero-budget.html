    Part 2 of AI Film School · Claude + free tools

    Make a film with Claude and free tools

    Part 1 used a paid studio and thousands of generations. This part does the same job with almost no money: Claude writes and builds, and free tools fill the gaps. The craft from Part 1 still applies. Only the way pictures are made changes.

    Pick your path first

    Claude cannot generate photos or video clips. It writes, plans, and builds animation as code. That gives you three paths:

    Path A · Claude only

    Code film

    Claude builds the whole film as code: shapes, light, type, 3D and sound. A free renderer turns it into an MP4.

    • Fully free (apart from your Claude plan)
    • No watermark, no daily limit
    • Best for: animation, mood pieces, explainers, music videos, ads with type
    • Weak at: realistic people and animals
    Path B · Claude + free AI

    Clip film

    Claude is your writer and director. Free daily credits on AI image and video tools make the shots.

    • Realistic look possible
    • Slow: about one short clip a day per tool
    • Watermarks and licence limits on many free plans
    Path C · Mix

    Hybrid

    Use a few AI or free stock clips for the realistic moments. Claude's code does titles, transitions, maps, graphics and sound.

    • Spends free credits only where they matter
    • Often the best result for zero money

    Rule of thumb: if your story needs a real face on screen, go B or C. If it can be told with light, shapes, objects, places, text and sound, go A. You get unlimited retries.

    Lesson 01

    Choose an idea your tools can make

    WhyWith no budget, the idea has to fit the tools. A good fit looks rich. A bad fit looks cheap however hard you try.

    1. Tell Claude your idea in one or two sentences, and which path you picked.
    2. Ask: "What will be hard to make with these tools? Give me 2–3 ways to tell the same story that suit them."
    3. Keep the subject and change how it is shown. A lonely old man can become his empty chair, his window light and his voice.
    4. Pick a length. Start with 30–60 seconds. Short and finished beats long and half-made.

    Code is good at

    • Light, colour, weather, water, particles
    • Objects, rooms, cities, maps, space
    • Type, diagrams, data, interfaces
    • Stylised or silhouette characters

    Code is bad at

    • Realistic faces and bodies acting
    • Realistic animals
    • Long talking scenes on camera
    Lesson 02

    Write the beats, second by second

    WhyA plan with seconds in it is what turns "a nice idea" into a film Claude can actually build.

    1. Ask Claude for a one-line logline, then a list of beats: what happens, from second X to second Y.
    2. Check every beat changes something you see or hear. A beat that changes nothing gets cut or given a job.
    3. The first 2 seconds must move and start with an action. No still title, no fade from black.
    4. Write any words (titles, narration) yourself or check them out loud. Remove lines that sound like an ad or like AI ("not X, but Y", "whispers", "a symphony of").
    Copy this to Claude

    I want to make a 45-second film with [code only / free AI clips / a mix]. Idea: [your idea]. Write a logline, then beats with exact seconds (0.0–3.5 s: …). Rules: the first 2 seconds move and start with an action; every beat changes something seen or heard; one action per beat; say what we hear in each beat. Flag anything my tools will struggle with.

    Lesson 03

    Set one look before anything moves

    WhyMoving video is slow to make and fix. A still frame shows a wrong look in seconds.

    1. Ask Claude for a director card: palette (4–6 colours), fonts, light, camera style, transitions, and a reason for each from this story.
    2. Path A: ask for one or two stills (single frames) of key moments and judge them. Path B: generate keyframe images first.
    3. Fix the look on stills. Only then make anything move.

    Do

    • Pick fonts and colours with a reason
    • Use the 60/30/10 colour split from Part 1
    • Ask "could this frame belong to another film?" If yes, redo it

    Don't

    • Accept default looks: purple gradients, neon, glowing lanterns, lighthouses, unless the story needs them
    • Let every shot look the same. Change size and camera move from shot to shot
    Lesson 04

    Path A: let Claude build the film in code

    WhyCode has no credit meter. You can change one number and render again as often as you like.

    The quick way (free claude.ai plan)

    1. Paste your beats and director card into Claude. Ask for "one HTML file that plays the film in the browser, with Web Audio sound".
    2. It appears as an artifact you can play. Give notes as numbers: "beat 3 is 1 second too long", "camera pushes in 20% slower".
    3. To get a video file, screen-record the playing page (macOS: Cmd+Shift+5; Windows: Win+Alt+R with Xbox Game Bar). Quality depends on your computer.

    The proper way (Claude Code on your computer)

    1. Install the free tools: Node.js, Python, Google Chrome, ffmpeg.
    2. Ask Claude Code to build the film as a web page where every frame is a pure function of time. The same frame always looks the same, so it renders without jitter.
    3. Claude renders a half-size draft frame by frame with headless Chrome and joins them with ffmpeg. You watch and give notes.
    4. Up to two rounds of notes on drafts, then the full-size final.
    5. A ready-made film-from-code skill (such as cinecode) does these steps for you, with checks and gates.
    Copy this to Claude Code

    Build this film as one web page: [paste beats + director card]. Every frame must be a pure function of time (frame index → picture; seeded randomness; assets loaded before the first frame). Show me 2 stills of key moments first. After I approve, render a half-size draft MP4 with headless Chrome + ffmpeg, with sound, and tell me which seconds to listen to.

    Lesson 05

    Path B: make shots on free daily credits

    WhyFree credits buy roughly one short clip a day per tool. Every try has to count.

    1. Ask Claude to turn the beats into a shot list, one short shot per line, with a style prefix on top (Part 1, Lesson 02).
    2. Make a keyframe image for each shot first. Images are cheaper than video, and the video copies the image's light and mistakes.
    3. Use image-to-video with that keyframe, not text-to-video. You get more control and fewer wasted credits.
    4. Spread the work over days and over several tools' free credits. Keep a list: shot, tool, date, result.
    5. Keep shots short (3–5 s). In editing, cut on movement to hide changes between tools.

    Do

    • Fix the prompt in Claude before spending credits again
    • Use the best 1–2 seconds of a failed clip
    • Read each tool's free-plan terms: watermark, resolution, commercial use

    Don't

    • Plan scenes that need the same face in 20 shots
    • Count on free limits staying the same
    • Upload other people's faces or brands
    Lesson 06

    Path C: mix code with real or AI shots

    WhyA few strong real-looking shots plus clean code graphics often look more finished than either alone.

    1. Mark in the beat list which moments need realism (a face, a hand, a real street). Everything else becomes code.
    2. Get those few shots from free stock (Pexels, Pixabay), your own phone, or free AI credits.
    3. Give the clips to Claude Code. It can put them inside the code film: titles over them, colour matching, transitions, maps, split screens.
    4. Match the look: ask Claude to grade code scenes and clips to the same palette and grain.

    Your own phone is a free camera. Ten seconds of real rain on a window, filmed by you, beats a minute of generated rain.

    Lesson 07

    Sound for free

    WhyViewers forgive simple pictures. They don't forgive bad sound.

    1. Add sound from the first draft: background sound (room, street, wind) per scene, plus effects on the exact frame they happen.
    2. Music: pick a real track with an open licence (Free Music Archive, Pixabay Music, YouTube Audio Library), or ask Claude to write simple music in code.
    3. Voice: best is your own voice on a phone, in a quiet room. Free machine voices: macOS say, or Kokoro / Piper on your computer.
    4. Never use a raw machine voice. Ask Claude to treat it: slower pace, small pauses, a little room echo, EQ.
    5. Make the final loudness about −14 LUFS so it plays well on YouTube and phones. Claude can do this with ffmpeg.

    Important: Claude can measure audio but cannot hear it. You must listen to every draft, with headphones.

    Lesson 08

    Edit and finish

    WhyEditing is where loose pieces become a film. The free editors are as good as paid ones for short work.

    1. Path A: the film comes out already edited. Ask Claude to change cuts by seconds.
    2. Path B / C: put clips on a timeline in DaVinci Resolve, Kdenlive, Shotcut or CapCut, with music from the start.
    3. Cut on movement. Use L-cuts (sound carries into the next shot). Never repeat the same transition twice in a row.
    4. Watch the film on your phone. Titles must be readable at that size.
    5. Export MP4 (H.264), 1080p, 24 or 30 fps.
    Lesson 09

    Review like a stranger

    WhyAfter many rounds you stop seeing problems. Part 1 used a "slop director". Alone, you need other tricks.

    1. Show it to one person who knows nothing about it. Ask them to tell you the story back. Where they get lost, fix.
    2. Ask Claude to look at stills from the draft and list what looks cheap or unclear. Give it the beat list so it can compare.
    3. Look frame by frame at cuts for flashes, jumps and pops.
    4. Rest one day, then watch once more with fresh eyes.
    Lesson 10

    Licences and sharing

    Why"Free to use" is not always "free for anything". One wrong track can get your video blocked.

    1. Keep a credits file from day one: every font, track, sound and clip, with its source and licence.
    2. Check if the licence asks for credit (CC BY), bans commercial use (NC), or allows anything (CC0).
    3. Check each AI tool's free-plan terms before posting or using the film for work.
    4. Put the credits at the end of the film or in the video description.

    Before you call it finished

    • The first 2 seconds move and start with an action.
    • Every beat changes something seen or heard.
    • One look throughout, but shot sizes and moves change.
    • No raw machine voice, no music you can't use.
    • You listened to it with headphones, all the way through.
    • Titles read on a phone screen.
    • Loudness about −14 LUFS.
    • A credits list for every font, track, sound and clip.

    Words to know

    BeatOne moment of the film where something changes, with start and end seconds.
    Director cardThe look plan: colours, fonts, light, camera and transitions, each with a reason.
    StillA single frame used to judge the look before rendering video.
    RenderTurning the code film into real video frames.
    Headless ChromeChrome running without a window, used to capture each frame.
    ffmpegA free command-line tool that joins frames and sound into an MP4.
    KeyframeThe image an AI video clip starts from.
    LUFSA loudness measure. About −14 is normal for online video.
    CC0 / CC BY / NCLicence types: no rules, give credit, no commercial use.
